## Speed and contact baseline

Group: Practice Foundations | drills

Filename: practice-speed-contact-baseline.mp3

Begin with a straight shot and center cue tip contact. Find the speed that produces a stop shot, then move the contact point higher for follow and lower for draw. Keep the object ball and pocket fixed while changing one setting at a time. Next, use a cut shot to compare the tangent line with the cue ball route. Before each shot, name the landing point you expect. Repeat each setup five times and record whether the cue ball finished short, long, left, or right.

More references:
- [Cue Ball Control — Target Practice (DrDaveBilliards)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-WdC_7MN0qI)

## Target landing precision

Group: Cue Ball Control | drills

Filename: practice-target-landing.mp3

Set up a repeatable object ball shot and choose a small cue ball landing zone. Begin with a generous target, then reduce it as your control improves. For this practice plan, use a three inch target and count a success only when the object ball is pocketed and the cue ball center finishes inside the target. Shoot ten attempts. Change only speed, then contact height, then the cut angle. Aim for eight successes out of ten before making the target smaller. Open More for Dr. Dave’s target practice demonstration.

More references:
- [Cue Ball Control — Target Practice (DrDaveBilliards)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=-WdC_7MN0qI)

## Rock Around the Clock

Group: Pattern Play | drills

Filename: practice-rock-around-clock.mp3

Open More and use Niels Feijen’s demonstration to set up Rock Around the Clock. Save the starting layout. Before every shot, choose the next object ball, its pocket, and the cue ball landing zone that leaves a useful angle. Prefer a route with room for a small speed error. Track pocketing and position separately. Complete one rotation first, then work toward three consecutive rotations without disturbing another ball. When you miss position, repeat the previous shot and compare a simpler route before changing the whole pattern.

More references:
- [Rock Around the Clock — Positional Play (Niels Feijen)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DAFJPeTSGOE)

## One-rail kick reference

Group: Kick Systems | drills

Filename: practice-one-rail-kick.mp3

Place an object ball behind a blocker so the cue ball needs one cushion to reach it. Open More for the diamond system tutorial. Identify the cue ball start, the rail aim point, and the intended object ball contact. Keep speed and spin consistent while testing the route. Repeat five attempts, moving the rail aim point by a small amount when the contact misses. Record the adjustment that works, then test a second starting location. Count legal object ball contact according to the game you are practicing.

More references:
- [Diamond System Formula — Kick Shots (Cue Craft Pro)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBFhoRZdGMI)

## Two-rail kick extension

Group: Kick Systems | drills

Filename: practice-two-rail-kick.mp3

Begin with a two cushion route that has space for the cue ball to travel. Mark the first rail aim point and predict the second rail contact before shooting. Keep the same speed and spin for each attempt. If the first contact is wrong, adjust the aim point. If the first contact is right but the final contact misses, examine the second rail route and speed. Record five attempts from the same start, then move the cue ball to a new starting diamond. Open More for the linked kick reference.

More references:
- [Diamond System Formula — Kick Shots (Cue Craft Pro)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=GBFhoRZdGMI)

## Corner-5 three-rail route

Group: Kick Systems | drills

Filename: practice-corner-five.mp3

Open More for Dr. Dave’s Corner-5 demonstration. Follow its starting, aiming, and arrival number scales together. Identify all three cushion contacts before you shoot. Start with a clear route, consistent speed, and the spin shown in the lesson. Compare the predicted arrival with the actual arrival, then make a small adjustment and repeat. Once the route is calibrated, add a target object ball and a blocker for escape practice. Test five different starting positions and record legal object ball contact separately from the final cue ball position.

More references:
- [Corner-5 Three-Rail System (DrDaveBilliards)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=G4dv9ekF3FY)

## Progressive difficulty benchmark

Group: Practice Benchmarks | drills

Filename: practice-progressive-benchmark.mp3

Open More for Sharivari’s progressive difficulty drill and use the demonstrated setup. Save the starting layout and record your first score. For your Trainer adaptation, begin with a comfortable position zone. After a successful set, reduce the zone or increase the shot difficulty, changing one condition at a time. Count both the pocketed ball and the required cue ball position. If the same error repeats, return to the last successful level. Record the highest level you can repeat, rather than only your best isolated attempt.

More references:
- [Progressive Difficulty Drill (Sharivari)](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=fNNtQM8WZcY)

## 105-minute practice session

Group: Practice Sessions | exercises

Filename: exercise-focused-practice-session.mp3

Use this one hundred and five minute practice plan. Spend thirty minutes on speed and target landing, aiming for eight successful pocket and position results out of ten with a three inch target. Spend forty five minutes on Rock Around the Clock, first completing one rotation and then working toward three consecutive rotations without disturbing another ball. Spend thirty minutes on kick escapes, starting with one rail and adding two and three rails as control improves. Test five selected escapes and aim for four legal contacts. Record the result at the end of each phase. If time runs out, save your layout and score, then continue there next session.

## Review, save, and repeat

Group: Practice Sessions | exercises

Filename: exercise-practice-review.mp3

At the end of practice, record attempts, pocketed balls, successful position results, and legal kick contacts. Note the most common miss and the single adjustment that helped. Save the table with a name that includes the drill, difficulty, and date. Download its table file if you want to share the same layout with an instructor or student. Begin your next session with that saved layout and the same scoring conditions. Improve the weakest result first, then increase the challenge once you can repeat the improvement.
