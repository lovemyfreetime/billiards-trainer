Measure your table

Use a clean ball and a level table. Film each trial side-on at 60 fps or faster with a measuring tape in view. Repeat each measurement at least three times, in both directions and on different cushions.

1. Cloth resistance

Gently roll a ball without side spin. Start measuring only after it rolls smoothly. Measure from that point until the ball stops, clear of every cushion. Record travel distance in metres and time in seconds. Count video frames for timing; a hand stopwatch adds substantial error.

2. Cushion response

Roll the ball squarely into a cushion with no side spin. From video, measure speed over short, equal windows immediately before and after impact. Enter the distance travelled in each window in metres. Use the same number of frames before and after. Avoid the impact frame, cushion compression and the first few frames of sliding after rebound; repeat at several gentle speeds.

The ratio of these distances estimates normal speed restitution. Rail-induced spin and cloth contact can bias it; this is a practical effective setting, not a laboratory cushion measurement.

Open Trainer with these measurements

These trials estimate rolling resistance and effective cushion response. They do not measure sliding friction, spin transfer, ball-ball throw, table level or pocket acceptance. Keep Trainer’s existing values for those.
